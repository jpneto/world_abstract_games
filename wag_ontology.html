An Ontology of Abstract Games

Games are related through the mechanisms (ludemes) they share. Each relation records that one game is a conceptual ancestor of another with respect to some ludemes; sometimes this reflects history, but not necessarily. Choose a game to see its ancestors and descendants. Click any game name to move to it.

Only relations passing the filter are followed. Plain terms keep relations with at least one of them. !term drops every relation that involves that term; with the box ticked, it drops only relations made up solely of negated terms. Click a ludeme in a tree to add it.

Ludeme glossary
expansion
Increase of size, dimensionality, or total number of playable positions of the board.
reduction
Decrease of size, like shrinking the board or disabling areas previously playable.
structure
A modification altering board adjacency, topology, symmetry or connectivity without necessarily changing its size.
setup
Alters the initial configuration of the game, like piece placement, asymmetry between players, or initial conditions.
piece-add
Introduces new piece types or increases the number of pieces. The added pieces may have new behaviors.
piece-rem
Removes piece types or reduces the number of pieces.
piece-change
Changes of movement, constraints, or the functioning of existing pieces; this includes promotion, transformation, or persistent state changes (like Chess castling).
placement
Alters how pieces are placed on the board. This might include multiple placements, or new effects on other pieces after placement.
movement
Alters how pieces move on the board.
capture
Adds capturing, or changes how pieces are captured or removed from play.
action
Adds, removes, or changes other categories of player actions. Examples include introducing passing, swapping, flipping.
turn
Alters the sequence, frequency, or structure of turns, like changes to alternation, forced moves, or multiple actions per turn.
win
Alters the criteria by which a game is won or drawn.
balance
A modification intended to improve competitive balance without introducing new mechanics. This includes restrictions, compensations (komi), bidding, asymmetric starting.
aesthetics
A change in appearance, usually for commercial or educational purposes.
